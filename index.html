The premise

Fiction gets to the danger first. So we send it in first.

Every real biosecurity failure was imaginable years before it was buildable. The gap between someone could and someone did is where defenders should live — and almost nobody does, because naming the threat feels like building it.

The Red Horizon Fellowship closes that gap with a discipline we call gonzo biosecurity: the writer who dreams up the threat is the same person who has to disarm it — in a safety-reviewed lab, in real time, in front of witnesses. Then we hand the antidote to the people who need it, and burn the recipe. Fiction with a footnote that reads: solved, shipped, disclosed.

The loop

Four phases. One law: leave it safer than you found it.

Every project runs the whole loop. A story is not finished until its countermeasure exists and works.

01

Horizon

Write the scenario. A piece of speculative fiction imagining one specific, plausible way biotech goes wrong — a failure mode, never a manual. Character and dread do the work.

Output: the story
02

Red Team

Adversarially tear the scenario apart with a biosecurity panel and a tabletop. Is it real? What's the actual attack surface? Which single assumption, if fixed, kills the whole plot?

Output: the threat model
03

Countermeasure

The gonzo sprint. Fellows hit the bench in a safety-reviewed community lab and build the defense — a detection assay, a screening hook, an open protocol, a policy ask — that neutralizes the whole threat class.

Output: the fix
04

Dispatch

Publish the fix and the story together, after biosafety review. The hazard stays redacted. The defense goes to every lab that can use it. The demo goes on the floor.

Output: the dispatch

The redaction law

We never publish operational hazard information — no sequences, synthesis routes, uplift methods, or step-by-step threat recipes. Every dispatch clears a biosafety review before release, and any hazardous detail is held under managed, responsible disclosure to the right authorities and vendors first. Publish the cure. Redact the recipe.

Deliverables

What a fellow actually ships

The Story

A gonzo dispatch

A finished piece of speculative fiction plus immersive, first-person field reporting — the anchor piece a general reader feels in the gut. Runs in the Red Horizon series.

The Defense

A working countermeasure

An open-source tool, wet-lab detection protocol, screening rule, or tabletop kit — demonstrated to work, released under a permissive license so any lab can pick it up.

The Record

A disclosure trail

A documented responsible-disclosure process: who got notified, what stayed dark, what changed. Signed off by the biosafety board before anything ships.

The Demo

A live red-team

A demonstration at a public venue — most often the DEF CON Biohacking Village — where the fix gets shown, stress-tested, and handed to the room.

The Brief

A one-page ask

Plain-language brief that turns the scenario into a specific demand on funders, regulators, or vendors: here is the gap, here is the fix, here is who pays for it.

The Kit

A reusable exercise

A facilitated tabletop or cold-open drill, packaged so any institution can run the same scenario internally — all tension, zero hazardous detail.

How a cohort runs

Sixteen weeks. Part-time. Remote-first.

Weeks 1–3 · Horizon

Scenario development

Craft sessions with biosecurity mentors. Each fellow drafts a scenario and a threat model. Wild imagination encouraged. Operational recipes never get written down. Not once.

Weeks 4–6 · Red Team

Adversarial review & tabletop

A mixed panel of scientists, security researchers, and ethicists tears each scenario apart for plausibility and names the load-bearing weakness to hit.

Weeks 7–12 · Countermeasure

The gonzo sprint

Bench and build time in safety-reviewed community labs, paired with a technical mentor. A 72-hour capstone turns the defense from prototype to demo.

Weeks 13–14 · Disclosure

Biosafety & disclosure review

The board reviews every artifact. Hazardous detail stays dark. Vendors and authorities get notified where relevant, with remediation time built in.

Weeks 15–16 · Dispatch

Publish & show

Story and fix ship together as a Red Horizon dispatch, with a live demonstration — most often at the DEF CON Biohacking Village.
